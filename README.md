# Thanks for checking my GitHub history

**If the green squares brought you here, they’ve done their job.**

Most of my professional work lives in private repositories and on client systems. In the age of AI, it’s easy to make a contribution graph look busy.

I’m an Engineering and Delivery Leader with 16+ years in software. I lead around 30 engineers and interview candidates from juniors to architects.

## If you’re hiring, ask about the work

An empty public profile is not a red flag. Most good engineers work under NDA. Ask candidates to walk you through one change they’re proud of. A product demo, a sketch, or an anonymized walkthrough all work.

- **What problem were you solving, and what did you personally own?**
- **Where did the change fit in the system?** Walk me through one real user flow, including what happens when something fails.
- **What alternatives did you consider?** Which constraints shaped your decision, and what trade-off did you accept?
- **How did you know it worked?** What did you learn after it shipped, and what would you design differently now?

### Same questions, a higher bar at each level

At every level I'm looking for the same thing: critical thinking and judgment. Can they explain *why*, name what else they considered, and show how they know it worked? What grows is the scope of what they can reason about.

| Level | Scope | Strong judgment looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Junior | Their own code | They explain every line, question what they're given (including AI output) before trusting it, and say "I don't know" honestly |
| Mid | A feature, end to end | They think through failure paths and edge cases, prove them with tests, and know when to ask for help |
| Senior | A system | Sound system design under real constraints: the trade-off they chose, what it cost, and how it behaves when things fail |
| Staff / Principal / Architect | Systems across teams | The same system design depth, applied to decisions that outlast them. They know what to say no to, and can explain it to a junior without losing accuracy |

Titles and degrees don't change the row. A PhD still has to show the judgment.

### Follow the answer, not the script

Keep asking “and then what?” until you reach the edge of what they know. Everyone has one. What happens there matters most. “I’m not sure, but here’s how I’d find out” is a strong signal. A confident guess presented as fact is not.

Watch for:

- “We” all the way down, never “I”
- Numbers with no story of how they were measured
- A project with no mistakes and nothing they’d change
- Being unable to go one level deeper than the slide

### If AI helped build it

Pick a part the AI wrote and ask them to explain it. What did it suggest that they rejected, and why? What did they verify themselves, and how would they debug it at 3 a.m.? Using AI is fine. Not understanding what it produced is not.

The ability to explain and challenge a design tells you more than a wall of green squares.

### Check this repo yourself

```bash
git log --format='%h  author: %ad  committer: %cd  %s' --date=short
```

The author dates go back to 2013. Every committer date is November 2026. Both can be set by hand, so neither proves anything. That’s the point.
